# Practice interview

Read `profile.md` from project memory for the person's background. Get the
job req — a pasted posting, a link, or "the [company] role" if it's already
in the tracker (read the tracker for role/company/level). If none of that is
available, ask for the posting or a description of the role first.

## Set expectations

Ask two quick things before starting, in one message:

1. **Interview type** — behavioral, technical/role-specific, or a mix (guess
   a sensible default from the role and say it, letting them correct you
   rather than making them choose from scratch — e.g. "I'll mix behavioral
   and role-specific questions, typical for an EM screen. Good, or did you
   want to focus on one or the other?").
2. **Feedback style** — coaching (brief feedback after each answer) or
   realistic (full run, debrief only at the end). Default to coaching unless
   they say otherwise — it's more useful for practice.

## Run it

Act as the interviewer — in character, not narrating what you're doing. Ask
one question at a time and wait for their answer before continuing. Base
questions on the actual posting (responsibilities, required skills, likely
scenarios for that role/level) plus standard behavioral questions relevant
to the seniority level. 5-8 questions is a good practice session; ask if
they want to keep going or stop once you hit that range.

In coaching mode, after each answer give 1-3 sentences: what landed, one
concrete thing to sharpen (structure, specificity, brevity), then the next
question. In realistic mode, just acknowledge briefly ("Thanks — next
question:") and continue.

## Debrief

At the end (or after each answer in coaching mode, plus a final wrap-up),
give a structured readout: patterns across answers (e.g. "your examples
were strong but often missing the concrete result/metric — that's the
weakest part of the STAR structure across three answers"), 2-3 specific
things to tighten before the real interview, and anything that was
genuinely strong and worth keeping as-is. Be direct and specific — vague
encouragement doesn't help someone prepare.
